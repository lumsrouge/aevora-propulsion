## Peak

**Peak:** ignition in the final worldflight leg.

It receives the strongest asset, the only warm saturated light, the quiet hold before it, and the final landing viewport. It must be visibly larger than every previous change and must resolve rather than fade away.

## Grammar

**Chosen grammar: Continuous World.** The request is literally one physical build in one place. The page therefore uses one fixed worldflight stage, one fixed copy layer, one empty spacer, and no normal scroll acts or hard section boundaries.

Why the other seven lose:

- **Filmic one-shot:** linear but introduces act boundaries, and it is the overused default.
- **Chaptered editorial:** deliberately cuts between chapters, contradicting the chosen continuity.
- **Live surface:** designed around an operable product interface, not a physical assembly.
- **Typographic poster:** bans the photographic engine that is central to the brief.
- **Gallery:** organizes multiple objects, while this story is one object becoming complete.
- **Split stage:** needs a persistent two-sided comparison the brief does not have.
- **Rhythmic cutlist:** builds its energy from hard cuts, the opposite of this world.

## Signature move: axial assembly rail

A page-local SVG cutaway acts as navigation and assembly ledger. Each waypoint locks another ring into position and keeps completed stages accumulated. Its marker travels from intake to exhaust. The stops are keyboard-focusable and jump to the correct scroll offset. During ignition the resolved cutaway closes around the CTA as a restrained status ring.

The rail is driven by `--sc-seg`, `--sc-segp`, and `sc:waypoint`; the shared engine remains untouched. It publishes a meaningful rendered-state signature for verification rather than raw scroll percentage.

## Reliability boundary

Kling can constrain head and tail frames but cannot enforce CAD geometry, component identities, or causal mechanical paths between them. The promise is a polished progressive visual assembly, not an engineering-accurate exploded-view simulation. If an assembly clip still morphs or teleports components after its allocated reroll, stop spending and use a deterministic transition between accepted anchors. The final ignition is safer because the engine topology remains fixed.

# Addendum, 2026-09-07

The build was resumed after a session reset. The interview record above stands
unchanged. Three things were decided or corrected in this session, and they are
recorded here rather than edited into the original answers.

## 1. The camera goes inside (user decision)

The original brief locked a fixed-scale camera on all four anchors to guarantee
invisible seams. That was an authored decision and was never put to the user,
while the user's own words ask for something else:

> "La camera dois nous amener dans l'intimité et le secret de l'assemblage et la fabrication."

Asked directly this session, the user chose:

> **"Ajouter un vrai plan rapproché"** — 4 legs: wide assembly, wide assembly,
> a slow push INTO the engine (combustor, compressor stages), then pull back and
> ignite as the peak.

The user also raised the credit ceiling to make it possible.

## 2. The peak gets the longest leg (score correction)

Three equal 2.34vh legs contradicts feel.md §5 for a worldflight, which asks the
peak to carry the page's shape by being the single long leg. Ignition is now a
ten-second clip at the same scroll rate, so it holds twice the span of any other
leg. Rate is held at 0.46vh per second of film across all four legs, spread 0.6%.

## 3. The silence is authored into the clip, not into `data-sc-linger`

`lingerEase` settles a leg at its **midpoint**, not at its tail, so it cannot
produce a hold before the peak. The silence is authored twice instead: leg 3's
camera decelerates to a near-rest in its final second, and no copy block is open
between track fractions **0.545 and 0.655**. That stretch is roughly 1.3
viewport-heights of deliberate quiet immediately before ignition. It is not dead
scroll: the film advances throughout, and the harness confirms it.

## Known limitation in the peak asset

The accepted ignition clip shows flame at the intake for roughly its middle
third. On a real turbofan that reads as a hot start or a compressor stall rather
than a successful first run. One reroll was spent against it; the reroll came
back worse (the camera pushed in instead of withdrawing, the test cell grew
invented lighting, and the intake flame was stronger), so the first take was
kept. This is exactly the boundary the original brief anticipated: the model can
be held to a start frame and a camera move, but not to a causal airflow model.
Fixing it needs another targeted generation, and that spend was not taken.

## Mobile is a different composition, not a shrink

the upper screen with the copy holding the lower screen on the page ground and
